# Telescoping Antenna Mast

A print-in-place telescoping mast for a small 915 MHz antenna. One print is five nested tubes, 250 mm tall, that pull out to about 1.1 m. Two prints joined by the coupler reach about 2.2 m.

## Printing

- **Mast section:** print standing, as it comes. It needs a printer with 250 mm of Z, such as the Qidi Q2 (270 × 270 × 256). It won't fit an Adventurer 5M.
- **Coupler:** print small end down.
- PETG, no supports, no brim. A brim would weld the tubes together at the foot. Each tube wall is 1.26 mm (three 0.42 mm lines), and the tubes are 0.4 mm apart. If the first layer squishes enough to fuse neighbouring rings, turn on elephant-foot compensation. Every foot already has a 0.4 mm chamfer.
- Print slowly enough that the tall thin inner tube doesn't wobble into its neighbour.

After printing, check that each tube turns freely in the one outside it before you pull anything out.

## Raising and lowering

Each inner tube has three lugs above its thick foot. The top of each outer tube has three teeth, and each tooth has a dip between two posts.

1. **Raise:** pull the section straight up until it stops, then twist it 60° either way and let go. The lugs drop 1.5 mm into the dips and carry the weight. The posts stop it turning back.
2. **Lower:** lift the section to the stop, twist it 60° until the lugs line up between the teeth, then push it down.

Raise the innermost tube first, then the next one out. Pulling the extended tube drags the next one up with it. Grip the newly exposed tube to twist it.

## Joining two prints

The outer tube's foot is a male thread, about 49 mm. The inner tube's top is a smaller male thread flush with its outside, about 20 mm. The coupler takes the small thread from below and the big thread from above.

1. On the lower print, raise and lock the inner tube. The locked lugs keep it from spinning while you screw the coupler on.
2. Screw the upper print's foot into the coupler.
3. Raise the upper print's sections, then the lower print's.

Both threads have 45° flanks, so they print without supports. The foot thread is also what a base or stand will screw onto later.
