# Meshology L1 Holster

A holster for the Meshology L1 Meshtastic radio, printed in TPU 64D. The radio slides down into it from the top. The back, sides and floor are solid; the front is a big cutout so the controls stay visible and usable, with 8 mm of wall reaching in from each side (and 8 mm of sill above the floor) to hold the radio in. The edge of the front opening and the rim round the top are rounded over so they aren't sharp.

It bolts to a 3x2 Kanix belt clip. A matching desk stand holds the radio up on a desk.

## Printing

- TPU 64D, one piece, standing on its floor with the open top up. No supports. The only steep overhangs are the bends of the locking tabs and the tops of the screw holes.
- About 50 g of TPU.
- The wall is 3 mm all the way round, bottom corners included, except a 45° skirt where the rounded bottom meets the bed.
- Four 5 mm drainage holes in the floor, edges chamfered.

The top slopes from front to back. The rim drops at about 44° from the back to the front lips, which stop about 41 mm below the radio's top and keep sloping down toward the opening. That leaves the top of the radio out where you can grab it.

A U-notch in the right side (seen from the front) clears a USB-C plug in the radio's side port.

## Locking tabs

The back wall comes up over the back of the radio, following the rounded corners of its back face. Two 17 mm tabs across the top, from 23 mm in from the left edge (seen from the back) across to the right corner, rise 3 mm past the radio's top back edge, then bend forward over three quarters of the top back bevel to keep it in. The right one wraps all the way round the corner's curve, its bend following the curved bevel, and two loops side by side fill the corner under its end (right side, seen from the back: one behind the back, one on the side), holes upright, for a cord or bungee looped over the radio's GPS antenna wing so the radio can't fall out and get lost. A third, single loop sits in the same corner three quarters of the way down. A guard stands beside the row on the left, level with the tabs' tops and with its outer corner cut off at 45°, so a strap or branch can't catch them. Slits, rounded over at the edges like the rim, let each tab spring back: push the radio down and it clicks in; pull the tabs back to lift it out.

## Mounting

The back carries a pad for a 3x2 Kanix belt clip (three across, two up): six M5x10 button-head screws through the clip into 4.2 mm pilot holes. The pilot holes are 8 mm deep, running through the pad into the wall, and stop 0.8 mm short of the radio.

## Fit

The channel is the radio's shape plus 0.1 mm on every face (`tolerance`; 0.3 printed too loose in PETG), including its rounded corners (R11 seen from the front) and the bevels round its front and back faces. The floor curves up into the corners and ramps up the bevels to meet them. The diagonal faces' size hasn't been measured yet (it's a 6 mm guess front-to-back per face), so check that first on a test print.

## Desk stand

A cradle cut to the same shape as the holster's pocket, leaning the radio back 15° so the front faces you. It holds the bottom 24 mm of the radio and has the same front opening as the holster, so the controls stay as easy to see and use. The cradle stops below the USB-C port, so you can charge the radio while it stands. The base runs 45 mm back behind the radio, so an antenna on top can't tip it over backwards. The walls across the front slope down into the cradle at 45°, so the radio slides in without catching. Every edge is rounded, the bottom one too.

Print it in PETG (or any rigid filament), standing on its base. No supports. About 61 cm³ of plastic, 72 × 60 × 35 mm.
